SWB vs LWB: which VW Transporter wheelbase is right for a campervan?

It's not just about size. Wheelbase affects your bed layout options, ferry pricing, daily drivability, and how many places you can actually park. Here's how to decide.

The short wheelbase versus long wheelbase debate happens quietly, without much fuss. But it's one of the most consequential decisions you'll make, because it affects bed length, storage, ferry costs, daily drivability, and how many car parks you'll actually fit into.

Most people frame it as a capacity question. "More space is better, right?" But that's not the full picture. A 405mm difference in load area doesn't sound like much until you're trying to fit a proper bed length, reversing into a tight space, or checking in for a ferry crossing that suddenly costs £40 more.

The numbers: what 405mm actually means

The VW Transporter SWB (short wheelbase) has a load area of 2,570mm. The LWB (long wheelbase) gives you 2,975mm. That's 405mm - roughly 16 inches.

In bed terms, this difference is significant. An SWB will fit a transverse bed across the back at around 1,450mm wide, but the length along the van is the constraint. With the wheel arch intrusion and the cab taking space, you're looking at roughly 1,850mm of usable length. That's 6'1".

If you're 5'10" or shorter, you're fine sleeping longitudinally (head to toe along the van). If you're taller, you're sleeping diagonally across the van, which works but isn't ideal night after night.

An LWB gives you approximately 2,250mm of usable length. That's 7'4". A proper longitudinal bed. An adult of any height sleeps straight. You can also fit a side bed along one wall without sacrificing the kitchen, or a rear transverse bed with more room either side.

What this means for your layout

In an SWB, your choices are limited. Most SWBs run one of two layouts:

  • RnR bed across the back (rear bed that folds up during the day) - compact, functional, leaves the space under the bed available for storage and systems. Works well for weekend trips.
  • Dinette with pop-up bed - gives you flexible seating, but the bed still has the length limitation issue.

In an LWB, you've got real options. You can run:

  • Longitudinal bed across the back - full-length, proper sleeping for two adults. The gold standard for couples or anyone over 6ft.
  • Side-facing bed along one wall - common in family builds, allows a child bed or additional seating opposite. Much more viable in LWB because you still have room for kitchen and storage forward.
  • Rear transverse bed with storage underneath - gives you more floorspace in the day and still a proper bed at night.
  • Premium layouts - wet bathroom, separation between sleeping and living space, luxury touches. The extra length makes these almost possible in SWB, genuinely comfortable in LWB.

The kitchen and storage story is similar. An SWB kitchen might be a two-burner hob, compact sink, and cupboards that don't quite hold everything. An LWB kitchen can be a proper galley with real counter space, three-burner hob, larger fridge, and dedicated storage for a week's worth of food.

Daily drivability: how different are they really?

Both vans drive like large cars. Smooth, stable, predictable. The difference is measurable but not dramatic.

The SWB overall length is approximately 4,900mm (16'1"). The LWB is approximately 5,300mm (17'4"). That's 400mm longer - less than a foot and a half. Not enormous.

In reality:

  • Lane width: Both fit comfortably in UK lanes. The LWB is noticeably tighter, but not impossible. You'll be aware of it. You won't be reversing.
  • Car parks: Standard spaces in supermarkets are about 2,600mm wide. Both fit. But compact spaces (2,400mm) are tighter in an LWB. The SWB will fit most compact spaces; the LWB might not.
  • Reversing: This is where length matters. An LWB is noticeably more difficult to reverse, especially into tight spaces. A rear reversing camera is almost essential on an LWB. Optional but helpful on an SWB.
  • Manoeuvrability: The SWB turns tighter. If you're regularly navigating narrow mountain roads or tight car park obstacles, the SWB feels lighter on its feet.
  • Fuel economy: The SWB is marginally better - roughly 1-2 mpg difference, depending on driving style and load. Not enough to justify the decision on its own, but worth noting.

The honest take: both drive beautifully. The SWB feels more agile. The LWB feels more planted and stable at speed. For most people, the difference isn't "difficult" - it's just "different."

The ferry question

This catches people off guard. Most UK ferries (especially to France) charge by vehicle category. An SWB Transporter is typically classified as a "standard van." An LWB often falls into "large vehicle" territory, depending on total length, height, and width.

The pricing difference: roughly £20-40 per crossing, depending on the ferry company and season. Return trip to France? That's £40-80 extra. Regular channel runs? It adds up.

Some ferry companies are more rigid about this than others. Brittany Ferries and Eurotunnel both have size thresholds - check before you commit to the LWB if ferrying is part of your plan.

Weight and insurance

An SWB is slightly lighter (the LWB body adds roughly 100kg unladen), which affects:

  • Payload capacity: If you're building a heavily spec'd campervan with solar, large battery, water systems, and two adults plus gear, the SWB payload is tighter. Check the van's MTPLM (maximum technically permissible laden mass) - Transporters are plated as T26/T28/T30/T32 (2.6-3.2 tonnes), and payload varies more by that rating than by wheelbase.
  • Towing: Not relevant for most campervans, but if you're planning to tow a trailer, the LWB has a higher limit.
  • Insurance: Marginal difference, usually £30-60 more per year for an LWB. Not a deciding factor.

The purchase price question

On the used market, an LWB typically commands a £1,000-3,000 premium over an equivalent SWB of the same age and mileage. That's because the market knows LWBs are more desirable for longer trips and family use.

New, the difference is similar - roughly 5-8% premium for the LWB depending on trim and spec.

Whether that premium makes sense depends entirely on how you'll use the van.

The answer for most buyers

Choose SWB if: You do weekend trips and short breaks. You're a couple (or solo) who value agility over space. You want to fit standard parking spaces without thinking. You want better fuel economy and lower ferry surcharges. You drive in tight UK lanes and narrow car parks regularly. You're willing to sleep diagonally or don't mind diagonal sleeping. You want lower purchase price and lower running costs.

Choose LWB if: You do extended trips (two weeks or longer). You're a family or two couples. You're taller than 5'10" and don't want to sleep diagonally. You want a proper kitchen and real storage. You'll spend 50%+ of your time in the van. You do regular ferry runs and don't mind the surcharge. You want more layout flexibility (side bed, multiple sleeping options, proper bathroom). You prioritise comfort over agility.

The van you want is the one that matches how you actually use it, not how you imagine you might use it. If you're realistic about weekend trips and solo travel, the SWB will save you money on purchase, parking, and ferries. If you're planning extended family adventures or full-time living, the LWB will repay its premium every time you don't have to sleep diagonally or compromise on kitchen space.
