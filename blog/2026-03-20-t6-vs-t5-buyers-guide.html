T5 or T6? The honest answer for first-time buyers

Most people ask this question the wrong way round. The answer isn't about the van — it's about where you live and how you drive.

When you start looking at VW Transporter campervans, the T5 vs T6 debate comes up within about 30 seconds. It's the first question forums argue about, the first question conversion companies field, and the first thing that will confuse you about budget versus compliance.

Here's the thing: the question shouldn't be "which is better?" It should be "which is right for my situation?" The answer depends almost entirely on one factor most first-time buyers forget to check before they start looking.

The only question that matters first: do you drive into a clean air zone?

The T5 (2009–2015) runs a Euro 5 diesel engine. It's brilliant, reliable, and available at significantly lower prices than equivalent T6 spec. But it's not ULEZ-compliant, which means it attracts a daily charge of £12.50 to drive in London's ULEZ, and similar charges in Bath, Bristol, Portsmouth, Birmingham, and a growing list of other UK cities.

The T6 (2015 onwards) runs a Euro 6 engine. It's ULEZ-compliant across all current UK clean air zones. It also introduced AdBlue — a diesel exhaust fluid you top up every 10,000 miles or so (roughly £15–25 and about five minutes of your life).

If you live in or regularly drive into London, Bath, Bristol, or Birmingham: buy a T6. The maths is simple. At £12.50 a day for a commuter who occasionally takes the van into the city, you'll spend hundreds of pounds a year on ULEZ charges — and those charges will only increase as more zones come online and charges rise. The T6 premium at purchase pays for itself within a year or two for many buyers.

If you live rurally, drive mostly on motorways and A-roads, and never go near a clean air zone: the T5 is a serious option. You get more van for your money.

What you actually get with a T6

Aside from ULEZ compliance, the T6 brought meaningful improvements over the T5:

  • Better engine calibration on the TDI units — particularly the 2.0 BiTDI 204PS, which pulls well and manages long-distance driving beautifully
  • Electric parking brake — a small thing that adds a lot of day-to-day usability
  • Improved infotainment — factory MIB2 head unit with DAB, reverse camera prep, and Apple CarPlay/Android Auto capability
  • Lane assist, adaptive cruise, and other driver aids on higher trim levels
  • Better DSG gearbox calibration on automatic models

None of this makes the T5 bad. It just makes the T6 better in ways that matter on long drives and in daily use.

What you actually get with a T5

The T5 has one significant advantage: value. At the same budget, you can typically buy a T5 with:

  • Lower mileage for the money
  • More conversion budget remaining
  • A longer conversion history from trusted companies (T5 conversions are well-documented and have a bigger support community)

The 2.0 TDI and 2.0 BiTDI engines in the T5 are proven across hundreds of thousands of miles. They don't need AdBlue. They're simpler. At high mileage with a good service history, a T5 TDI is one of the most reliable diesel vans ever made.

If you're self-building or doing a conversion, the T5 has a larger community of builds to reference. Parts are widely available and competitively priced.

The AdBlue question

A lot of first-time buyers read about AdBlue and worry about it more than they should. At £2 per litre and 10,000-mile refill intervals, it's a non-issue for most people. The T6 throws a dashboard warning at around 1,500 miles remaining — plenty of notice. Top up at a filling station or grab a 10-litre container online for about £10. Done.

The one thing to watch: running a T6 dry of AdBlue repeatedly is bad for the SCR (selective catalytic reduction) system. It's also an MOT fail if the system is malfunctioning. Keep on top of it and it's genuinely not a problem. Let it run dry repeatedly and you're looking at an expensive repair.

Budget reality check

Let's be direct about what your budget gets you in 2026:

  • Under £25,000: You're looking at T5s, probably with 100,000+ miles. Some good buys here — but inspect carefully and budget for new tyres and timing belt.
  • £25,000–£35,000: T5 Highline with reasonable mileage, or an entry-level T6 with higher mileage. This is where the ULEZ question really matters for your decision.
  • £35,000–£45,000: T6 Highline territory, potentially with lower mileage. Look at VW Approved Used — the 12-month warranty is genuinely valuable at this budget.
  • £45,000+: T6.1 (2019 onwards) becomes realistic. The 6.1 added a refreshed interior, improved safety systems, and 10.25" digital cockpit. Worth the step up if budget allows.

The honest answer

Buy a T6 if you drive within 50 miles of any UK city with a clean air zone or if you expect those zones to expand in your direction within the next five years. The premium is real but predictable — ULEZ charges are unpredictable and increasing.

Buy a T5 if you live rurally, never go near clean air zones, and want more van or more conversion budget for the same money. A well-maintained T5 at 120,000 miles with a full VW stamped service history is a better buy than a T6 at 60,000 miles with questionable paperwork.

The van you want is the one you'll still be happy driving in five years. For most UK buyers in 2026, that's a T6.
