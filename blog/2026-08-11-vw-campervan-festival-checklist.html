VW campervan festival checklist: what to pack, power and avoid

A festival campervan pitch looks simple until the field turns wet, the phone battery dies, and you realise the thing you forgot is the thing keeping you dry. This is the practical list.

Short answer: the festival setup that works

For a UK festival weekend, aim for a simple, self-contained setup: decent leisure battery, phone charging, a way to keep muddy kit outside the sleeping area, blackout or thermal window covers, and food that does not need a full kitchen. The best festival van is not the one with the most kit. It is the one you can pack down fast on Monday morning.

At a glance checklist

  • Power: leisure battery charged, USB/12V chargers, power bank, solar if you have it.
  • Sleep: proper bedding, earplugs, eye mask, window covers, spare blanket.
  • Mud control: doormat, boot tray, wet bag, microfibre towels, bin bags.
  • Food: cool box or fridge, easy breakfasts, one-pan meals, water carrier.
  • Weather: waterproof jacket, dry shoes, awning straps, warm layer for night.
  • Security: keep keys on you, hide valuables, do not leave gadgets charging visibly.

Power: keep it boring and reliable

Festival power demands are usually modest: phones, lights, maybe a fridge, possibly a diesel heater if the weather turns. You do not need a huge inverter for that. You do need enough 12V and USB charging points, because sharing one cigarette-lighter socket between four people gets old quickly.

If your van has solar, arrive with the leisure battery already full and treat solar as a top-up, not a miracle. Shaded fields, bad weather and roof boxes all reduce input. A separate power bank is still worth taking for the walk between the pitch and the stages.

Sleeping: comfort beats cleverness

Festival nights are noisy, damp and colder than the daytime forecast makes you think. Take normal bedding if you have space. A proper duvet and pillow will beat a thin sleeping bag every time. Add earplugs, an eye mask, and thermal covers or curtains so sunrise does not end your sleep at 5am.

If you are using a pop-top, check the weather before leaving it up overnight. Wind is the enemy. A cold, wet, flapping canvas roof is not fun at 3am.

Mud management is the difference between good and grim

The mistake is letting the van become the hallway, bedroom and boot room at the same time. Put a doormat or rubber mat outside the sliding door. Keep a boot tray just inside. Use one wet bag for coats and one bin bag for genuinely filthy shoes.

Microfibre towels are useful because they dry fast and pack small. Take more than one. One for people, one for the dog if relevant, one for wiping the floor and step.

Awning or no awning?

A retractable awning is useful for rain and shade, but only if it is properly pegged and strapped. Festival fields are exposed. If wind picks up, wind the awning in before you go to the arena. A broken awning is an expensive way to learn that lesson.

A simple awning rail and tarp can be enough for a weekend. The goal is a dry patch by the door, not a full campsite living room.

Food: plan for tired people, not Instagram

Do not build the weekend around complicated cooking. Festival food is expensive, but you will still buy some. The van food job is breakfast, late-night snacks and one fallback meal when nobody can face a queue.

  • Breakfast: porridge pots, pastries, bananas, coffee.
  • Lunch fallback: wraps, cheese, crisps, fruit.
  • Dinner fallback: one-pan pasta, chilli, noodles or soup.
  • Drinks: water carrier, refillable bottles, tea/coffee kit.

Security: boring habits work

Keep the van key on you, not hidden in the van. Do not leave laptops, cameras or power banks visible. If you are charging devices, tuck them away rather than leaving them glowing by a window. Most festival theft is opportunistic. Make your van less easy than the one next door.

What not to bother with

  • A huge inverter unless you already know why you need it.
  • Too much water if the festival has taps nearby — it is heavy and takes space.
  • Complicated cooking gear that creates washing up.
  • New, untested awning setups — practise before the field.
  • White trainers unless you enjoy regret.
