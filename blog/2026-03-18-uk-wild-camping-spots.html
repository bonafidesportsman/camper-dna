20 UK wild camping spots that work brilliantly in a VW van

From the Cairngorms to Pembrokeshire — spots that are accessible, genuinely beautiful, and tolerated (or explicitly legal) for overnight van stays.

One of the questions we get most often is: "Where do you actually go?" It's a fair question. Wild camping laws in England and Wales are complicated (short answer: it's technically not legal on most land without permission, but tolerated in many places). Scotland is different — right to roam legislation means wild camping is explicitly legal almost everywhere.

The spots below are places we've used, friends have recommended, or that have well-established van camping communities. None of them involve paying a campsite. All of them are accessible in a standard-height T5 or T6 (not a high-roof — I'll flag where low bridges are a factor).

One rule I try to follow: leave the spot cleaner than you found it. Pack out everything. Keep noise down after 10pm. These places stay available because van campers don't abuse them.

Scotland (right to roam — all legal)

1. Loch Lomond — Rowardennan

The eastern shore car park at Rowardennan fills up on summer weekends, but arrive Thursday evening and you'll often have the loch to yourself. Stunning sunrise views east. The walk up Ben Lomond starts from here if you want it.

2. Glencoe — Altnafeadh

The layby at Altnafeadh, at the head of the valley near the A82. Dramatic views, no facilities, genuinely remote feeling despite being accessible. Go in autumn for the full effect — the colours are extraordinary.

3. Cairngorms — Loch Morlich

The beach at Loch Morlich in the Cairngorms National Park is one of the genuinely surprising wild camping spots in the UK — pine forests, clear water, white sand. Paid car park but overnight van camping is tolerated outside peak summer weekends.

4. Isle of Skye — Elgol

The road down to Elgol is single-track and ends at a small car park with views straight to the Cuillins across Loch Scavaig. The most dramatic roadside view I've slept in front of. Check the road is passable in winter.

5. North Coast 500 — Achmelvich Beach

The layby above Achmelvich (not the main beach car park) provides private views of the beach and the Atlantic. Arrive in the evening to catch the light dropping over the water. Cold in spring — bring your diesel heater.

Wales

6. Pembrokeshire — St Non's Bay

A small layby above the bay near St Davids. Tolerated for overnight van stays — it's a popular walking area during the day, quiet at night. The sea views are exceptional and the headland walks are some of the best in Wales.

7. Brecon Beacons — Pont ar Daf

The Pont ar Daf car park at the foot of Pen y Fan. Early morning ascent before the day walkers arrive, then back to the van for breakfast. Paid parking during the day, but the overnight fee is minimal and the location is excellent.

8. Snowdonia — Llyn y Gadair

The layby on the road to the Minffordd Path on Cadair Idris. Quiet, tucked into the valley, and a straightforward drive from most of England. The walk up Cadair is serious but not technical — one of the best mountain days in Wales.

9. Ceredigion — Aberaeron to New Quay coast

Several cliff-top laybys along this stretch of coast offer sea views and quiet nights. Not a single location — drive the B4342/A487 and look for spots that feel right. The villages here are worth a morning visit too.

Lake District and Northern England

10. Hardknott Pass — Eskdale Valley

A small layby near the bottom of the Hardknott Pass (not the pass itself — that's no place for a van at night). The valley is quiet and the stone walls and fields here are exactly what you imagine when you think of the Lakes.

11. Kielder Forest — Deadwater Valley

Forestry England land in Kielder is managed for recreation, and overnight stays are generally tolerated in the forest car parks if you're not blocking access. The dark skies here are among the best in England — no light pollution.

12. Yorkshire Dales — Malham Cove

The car park at Malham is paid during the day but quiet at night. Short walk to the base of the cove or up to the limestone pavement. Summer evenings here are long and warm — good spot for a first van camping trip with non-van friends who need convincing.

13. North York Moors — Sutton Bank

The National Trust car park at Sutton Bank escarpment. Views west over the Vale of York on a clear day are extraordinary. Paid in daytime, tolerant overnight. The walk along the escarpment edge to Roulston Scar is excellent.

South West England

14. Dartmoor — Dartmeet

Dartmoor is one of the few places in England where wild camping is explicitly permitted in certain areas (check the current Dartmoor National Park guidance — the rules have been debated recently). Dartmeet has a small car park and access to the moor for walking and wild camping.

15. Cornwall — Gwennap Head

The National Coastwatch Institution station layby near Gwennap Head, with views of the Lizard and Land's End. Park here and walk the coastal path in either direction. Morning light on the sea from the van door is one of those views that makes the whole thing make sense.

16. Exmoor — Dunkery Beacon

The National Trust car park near Dunkery Beacon, the highest point on Exmoor. Good walking directly from the car park, and the heather moorland landscape is unlike anywhere else in England. Clear of midges, unlike Scotland.

17. Dorset — Dancing Ledge

A small layby on the approach road to Dancing Ledge, before the National Trust gate (check access restrictions). Morning walk to the ledge itself is 30 minutes each way and consistently one of the best short coastal walks in Dorset.

A note on van camping etiquette

These spots stay available because the people who use them behave well. That means:

  • No generator noise (this one is non-negotiable — get a good diesel heater and leisure battery instead)
  • Park in existing spots, not on grass or verges
  • Leave nothing behind — not even biodegradable waste
  • Move on in the morning — don't occupy a spot for multiple days
  • If a spot is posted with "no overnight camping" signs, respect them and find somewhere else

Van camping in the UK is a grey area in many places. The reason it works is that most people who do it are respectful. The handful who aren't are the reason councils put up bollards and rangers issue fines. Don't be that person.
