The Platform

T5, T6 & T6.1 — Know Your Van

Three generations, one platform, and a decision that shapes everything that comes after. Here's what actually matters.

T5 photo
2003 – 2015 Not ULEZ

VW T5

Proven, Plentiful, and Priced Right

Price guide£5k–15k
Engine1.9 / 2.0 TDI
EmissionsEuro 5
DSG availableFrom 2009

The van that started the modern campervan obsession. The T5 is abundant, affordable, and proven — which is exactly why it still dominates the conversion market. Buying one means your entire conversion budget goes further, with a huge parts and independent specialist ecosystem to support it.

The 2.0 TDI four-cylinder (introduced in the 2010 facelift) is the sweet spot: simpler than the older V6 units, strong enough for a loaded camper, and understood by any competent mechanic. Earlier 1.9 TDI engines are similarly bulletproof but show their age on motorways. As a camper, a well-built T5 is virtually indistinguishable from a newer van on the road — all the same layouts, bed systems, and roof options fit identically. The one hard reality: T5s are not ULEZ-compliant, so check coverage maps if London or other clean air zones are in your plans.

Best for

Buyers who want maximum conversion budget for a given total spend, or who aren't going anywhere near a ULEZ zone.

T6 photo
2015 – 2019 ULEZ compliant*

VW T6

The Industry Workhorse

Price guide£10k–22k
Engine2.0 TDI
EmissionsEuro 6*
DSG available150 / 204PS

A significant mechanical update on the T5 platform — same body, modernised engines, and Euro 6 emissions compliance. That last point is the critical one for most buyers. Check the V5 carefully: T6 models built before September 2016 are Euro 5. Euro 6 compliance only applies from that date onward.

The 2.0 TDI 150PS is the conversion sweet spot — strong, efficient, and available with DSG automatic on 150PS and 204PS variants (a genuine quality-of-life upgrade on long drives). The T6 is the current workhorse of the professional conversion industry: wide second-hand stock, high build quality, and the optional Sportline trim if you want lowered suspension and factory body kit from the outset.

Best for

Buyers who want Euro 6 compliance, modern running gear, and the broadest choice of second-hand stock at a sensible price.

T6.1 photo
2019 – present ULEZ compliant

VW T6.1

Current Gen — the Refresh That Matters

Price guide£18k–30k+
Engine2.0 TDI
EmissionsEuro 6
CarPlayHighline std.

Same platform as the T6, but with a meaningful cosmetic and tech refresh. The front face is sharper and more car-like, the digital cockpit is standard on higher trims, and Highline spec includes wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto — saving you a retrofit if you're keeping the factory dash.

As a camper the T6.1's conversion options are identical to the T6 — every roof kit, bed system, and furniture profile fits the same. For most buyers the premium over a clean T6 won't be justified by the conversion output. But if you want the newest-looking van on the drive and the factory infotainment to match, it's worth it. The one honest caveat: there's less second-hand stock at sensible prices compared to T6, so you may end up paying more for fewer options.

Best for

Buyers prioritising current-generation aesthetics, factory infotainment tech, and who have the budget to suit.

The Dimension Decision

Long Wheelbase vs Short

The single most important decision before anything else is fixed. Get this right first.

Short Wheelbase

SWB

Overall length ~4,890mm · Load area ~2,600mm

4,890mm
  • Fits standard UK multi-storey car parks
  • Noticeably easier to manoeuvre on narrow campsites and country lanes
  • Parks like a large car in everyday life
  • Full-length bed, kitchen, and storage all fit with a well-planned layout
  • Wider choice of second-hand stock and professional conversion experience

The most popular choice for weekends-and-holidays use. Efficient layouts make the 2,600mm load area more than enough for most people.

Long Wheelbase

LWB

Overall length ~5,290mm · Load area ~3,000mm

5,290mm
  • ~400mm more in the load area — enough for a rear seating area alongside the bed
  • More generous kitchen runs and easier to incorporate a wet room
  • Better suited to full-time or extended travel
  • Easier to carry a fourth person without compromising the layout

The right choice if you need the space — but the compromise is real. Noticeably more awkward in towns, tight campsites will cause friction, and you'll feel the length every time you park in a city.

Verdict

Most people buying a weekends-and-holidays campervan are better served by a well-planned SWB. Go LWB if you're frequently travelling with four people, want a dedicated toilet cubicle, or are planning extended travel — not because you vaguely feel like more space is better.
