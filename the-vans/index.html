The Platform

T5 to T7 — Know Your Van

Four generations, two platforms, one decision that shapes everything that comes after. Here's what actually matters.

VW has been making the Transporter since the 1950s — the T2, T3, and T4 all have devoted followings and the occasional conversion project. But the modern conversion market runs almost entirely on T5 onwards. They share the same essential body dimensions, the same conversion ecosystem, and the same parts supply chain, which means roof kits, bed systems, furniture profiles, and specialist knowledge are all freely transferable between generations. That's what this page covers — plus a note on the T7, which takes the nameplate in a different direction entirely.

2003 – 2015 Not ULEZ

VW T5

Proven, Plentiful, and Priced Right

Price guide£10k–30k
Engine1.9 / 2.5 / 2.0 TDI
EmissionsEuro 4 / 5
DSG availableFrom 2010 (T5.1)

The van that started the modern campervan obsession. The T5 is abundant, affordable, and proven — which is exactly why it still dominates the conversion market. Buying one means your entire conversion budget goes further, with a huge parts and independent specialist ecosystem to support it.

The 2.0 TDI four-cylinder (introduced in the 2010 T5.1 facelift) is the sweet spot: simpler than the older five-cylinder 2.5 TDI, strong enough for a loaded camper, and understood by any competent mechanic. Earlier 1.9 TDI engines are similarly bulletproof but show their age on motorways. Pre-facelift vans (2003–2009) are Euro 4; the T5.1 facelift (2010–2015) brought Euro 5, but neither generation is ULEZ-compliant — check coverage maps if London or other clean air zones are in your plans.

Best for

Buyers who want maximum conversion budget for a given total spend, or who aren't going anywhere near a ULEZ zone.

2015 – 2019 ULEZ compliant*

VW T6

The Industry Workhorse

Price guide£20k–45k
Engine2.0 TDI
EmissionsEuro 6*
DSG available150PS (opt) / 204PS (std)

A significant mechanical update on the T5 platform — same body, modernised engines, and Euro 6 emissions compliance. That last point is the critical one for most buyers. Check the V5 carefully: vans built before the summer 2016 factory changeover are Euro 5 (N1 commercial vehicles weren't required to meet Euro 6 until September 2016). Euro 6 compliance only applies from that date onward.

The 2.0 TDI 150PS is the conversion sweet spot — strong, efficient, and available with the 7-speed DSG automatic (standard on the 204PS BiTDI, optional on the 150PS). The T6 remains the workhorse of the professional conversion industry: broad second-hand stock, proven build quality, and the optional Sportline trim if you want lowered suspension and factory body kit from the outset.

Best for

Buyers who want Euro 6 compliance, modern running gear, and the broadest choice of second-hand stock at a sensible price.

2019 – 2024 ULEZ compliant

VW T6.1

The Last True Transporter

Price guide£22k–40k+
Engine2.0 TDI
EmissionsEuro 6
CarPlayHighline std.

Same platform as the T6, but with a meaningful cosmetic and tech refresh — and the last generation built on VW's own Transporter architecture before the T7 moved to a shared Ford platform. The front face is sharper and more car-like, the digital cockpit is standard on higher trims, and Highline spec includes wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto — saving you a retrofit if you're keeping the factory dash. Production ended in 2024.

As a camper the T6.1's conversion options are identical to the T6 — every roof kit, bed system, and furniture profile fits the same. Second-hand stock has grown significantly as lease returns come through, so there's now more choice than the T6 at the upper end of the market. The premium over a clean T6 is real, but if you want the newest-looking van on the drive with the factory tech to match, this is the sweet spot.

Best for

Buyers prioritising current-generation aesthetics, factory infotainment tech, and who have the budget to suit.

T7 photo
Unveiled 2024 — On sale 2025 ULEZ compliant

VW T7 Transporter

The Next Generation — Barely Here Yet

Price (new)From £31k +VAT
PlatformFord Transit Custom
PowertrainsDiesel / EV (PHEV 2026)
Engine2.0 TDI 109–168PS

The T7 Transporter was unveiled in 2024 and is co-developed with Ford, sharing the Transit Custom platform — a significant departure from VW's own Transporter lineage. UK deliveries began in April 2025 and it's available with diesel (109, 148, or 168PS) or fully electric powertrains, with a plug-in hybrid following in 2026. An 8-speed auto is standard on the 168PS. Note: the T7 is physically larger than the T5/T6 — the SWB is ~5,050mm, roughly 150mm longer than a T6 SWB.

From a conversion standpoint, the T7 is still a young platform. The conversion ecosystem built around T5/T6/T6.1 — the pop-top roofs, the RIB bed systems, the furniture profiles, the specialist converter knowledge — doesn't carry over to a different platform. Early converters like Redline and Spartan are now offering T7 builds, but established kits and proven conversion templates are still limited. If you're buying in 2026 and specifically want a brand-new T7, go in knowing you're among the first wave.

Best for

Buyers who want a brand-new van with modern powertrain options, and are comfortable being among the first to commission a conversion on the new platform.

The Dimension Decision

Long Wheelbase vs Short

The single most important decision before anything else is fixed. Get this right first.

Short Wheelbase

SWB

Overall length ~4,900mm · Load area ~2,570mm

4,900mm
  • Fits standard UK parking bays and height barriers (base van — pop-top adds ~400mm)
  • Noticeably easier to manoeuvre on narrow campsites and country lanes
  • Parks like a large car in everyday life
  • Full-length bed, kitchen, and storage all fit with a well-planned 2,570mm layout
  • Wider choice of second-hand stock and professional conversion experience

The most popular choice for weekends-and-holidays use. Efficient layouts make the 2,570mm load area more than enough for most people.

Long Wheelbase

LWB

Overall length ~5,300mm · Load area ~2,975mm

5,300mm
  • ~400mm more load length — meaningful extra room for layout flexibility
  • More space for a longer kitchen run, separate storage, or a fixed rear bed
  • Easier to live in for extended trips or full-time use
  • More practical if you regularly carry bikes, boards, or bulky gear inside

The right choice if you need the space — but the compromise is real. Noticeably more awkward in towns, tight campsites will cause friction, and you'll feel the length every time you park in a city.

Verdict

These dimensions apply to T5, T6, and T6.1 — all share the same body. The T7 is larger: its SWB is ~5,050mm (roughly the length of a T5/T6 LWB). Most people buying a weekends-and-holidays campervan are better served by a well-planned SWB. Go LWB if you're frequently travelling with four people, want a dedicated toilet cubicle, or are planning extended travel — not because you vaguely feel like more space is better.
