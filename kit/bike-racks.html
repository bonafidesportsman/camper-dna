Kit & Gear Bike Racks & Towbars

Bike racks & towbars

The towbar-mounted rack is by far the best solution for carrying bikes on a T5 or T6. It keeps the roof clear, loading is easy, and the right rack swings out around barn doors without you needing to remove it.

Barn doors vs tailgate — it changes the rack choice

If you have barn doors, you need a rack that either swings open with the doors or clears them entirely when the doors open. The Atera Strada Vario is the go-to here — it folds and swings out to the side, so you can open the barn doors fully without removing the rack. Most other towbar racks require you to unhitch or slide them to one side, which is annoying when you're doing it several times a day at a campsite.

Tailgate owners have more flexibility — any towbar rack works, since the door swings up rather than out. The Thule EPos is worth the premium if you're carrying e-bikes or want tool-free loading.

The towbar question

You need a towbar before a rack makes sense. For T5 and T6, the Westfalia detachable is the standard recommendation — genuine quality, detachable when not needed, and compatible with all the main rack manufacturers. A fixed towbar is cheaper but looks wrong on a van you've invested in. The detachable coupling takes 30 seconds to remove.

Specify the towbar with 13-pin electrics (not 7-pin) — the rack's indicator and brake lights need it, and some newer racks require 13-pin for the weight sensing system.

Roof racks

If you're a surfer or need to carry boards, kayaks, or ladders, a roof rack is the answer — but it raises the van's height, which matters if you have a pop-top (they're often not compatible). Check your pop-top manufacturer's guidance before ordering. For T6 Highlines with a factory roof rail, Thule and Reimo both make purpose-fit systems.

Always check compatibility with your specific vehicle before buying. Van models, years, and conversion specs vary — we can't guarantee every product fits every setup. If in doubt, contact the manufacturer or your converter.
